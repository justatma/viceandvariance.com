THE MEETING

Two ads. Three orders. One very confident founder.

You’ve been in that meeting. The dashboard says 2.0% versus 1.7%, displayed to two decimal places, as if the difference were a settled fact about the future. Someone declares a winner. The budget moves.

Then next week arrives, and the “winner” doesn’t.

But it keeps happening. The lousy afternoon that becomes a “collapsing campaign.” The $300 test that was never going to answer anything. The request to “tighten up” the uncertainty, as if it were a formatting choice.

Here’s the possibility: what if you could look at the same numbers and say, precisely and calmly, how much they are worth? Not a vibe. A number, with a range, attached to the question that actually matters.

Now picture the other meeting. Someone says the data is clear. You say: here is what we measured, here is what we can infer, here is what we can’t know yet, and here is what it would cost to find out. And the room believes you, because you can show them the floor.

That room is why this book exists.

THE METHOD

The book’s method in five moves.

  1. 01Define the decision before the math. A beautifully computed probability about the wrong target still funds the wrong campaign.
  2. 02Attach probability to the business threshold. Break-even CPA, not the vanity metric the dashboard volunteered.
  3. 03Price the test before you run it. Sometimes the honest answer is that the answer costs more than it’s worth.
  4. 04Keep measured, inferred, and caused in separate buckets. The whole book is this distinction, exercised until it’s reflex.
  5. 05Let AI propose; let checks decide. Four AI-assistant tasks, and a failure test to run against any AI analyst before you trust it.

You’ll follow North Pier from a two-day overreaction to a decision memo a founder can defend after the outcome arrives, including the uncomfortable moment where a 99.1% probability still isn’t a reason to move the whole budget.

The next time someone says the data is clear, you’ll know exactly which question it actually answered.
